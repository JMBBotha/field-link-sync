# Terms & Conditions Review — Legal Risk Notes

Reviewed against the South African Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008 (CPA) — particularly section 48 (unfair/unreasonable/unjust terms), section 49 (risk-limiting terms must be conspicuously flagged), and section 51 (terms that are prohibited outright, including any exclusion of liability for gross negligence). ([SAFLII, Consumer Protection Act consolidated text](https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/cpa2008246/))

## Weak areas and suggested fixes

- **2.1 Full upfront payment for the unit.** Requiring 100% payment for goods "as acceptance of our quotation" before any work starts is common in the trade, but is a term the CPA/Tribunal will scrutinise if invoked adversely to a consumer (s48(2): terms "excessively one-sided" or "so adverse... as to be inequitable"). Fix: state clearly what happens if a delay or cancellation occurs after this payment (refund timeline, restocking fee if any) so it isn't left silent — silence tends to be read against the drafter.
- **2.3 "It will be assumed" the client is responsible for payment.** Vague and one-sided — "assumed" language gives you unilateral discretion to decide who owes money, which brushes against the s48/s51 "sole discretion" grey-list category (terms that let the supplier alone decide compliance or interpretation). Fix: name the payer explicitly on the quote/invoice itself rather than relying on an assumption clause.
- **5.1/5.2 Warranty tied to a "service contract".** Making the compressor's extended warranty conditional on an ongoing paid service contract is fine commercially, but nowhere does the document say this is *in addition to* (not instead of) the consumer's statutory implied-warranty/right-to-return protections (CPA ss55–56, 6-month right to return defective goods). Without that carve-out, a court could read 5.1/5.2 as an attempt to contract out of statutory rights, which s51 voids outright. **Fixed this session** — added clause 5.3: "This warranty is in addition to, and does not replace or limit, any rights you have under the Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008."
- **No Errors & Omissions clause existed.** There was no protection at all against liability for a typo, pricing slip, or a manufacturer's own published spec being wrong. **Fixed this session** — added a new "Errors and Omissions" section (6.1–6.3) that: (a) limits liability for genuine clerical/admin errors only if corrected promptly and never results in overcharging, (b) explains reliance on manufacturer-published specs, and (c) explicitly carves out gross negligence, fraud and wilful misconduct plus preserves all CPA rights — this carve-out is required because s51 makes any attempt to exclude liability for gross negligence void per se, so an E&O clause without it would risk being struck down entirely.
- **3.1/3.2 Electrical Work wording is genuinely confusing**, not just a legal risk but a clarity one: the two sentences about capacity ranges read as fragments ("For units with capacities of 24000Btu - 60000Btu. For units with capacities of 9000 - 18000, a suitable electrical/plug point needs to be provided.") — unclear which capacity range needs what. Recommend rewriting for plain language, since CPA s48/s22 also weighs "plain language" drafting as a fairness factor.
- **8.1 Confidentiality clause doesn't name POPIA.** You collect personal information (addresses, contact details, banking references) but the clause only promises general "confidentiality," not compliance with the Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013. **Fixed this session** — added a reference to POPIA in clause 9.1 (renumbered from 8.1).
- **9 (now 10) Termination — no cancellation/refund mechanics.** "Either party may terminate... with written notice" doesn't say what happens to any deposit paid, or address the CPA's rules on estimates (consumers can generally cancel an unstarted job and are only liable for reasonable costs actually incurred). Recommend adding an explicit clause: if the client cancels before work starts, they are only liable for costs already reasonably incurred (e.g. non-refundable special-order stock), not the full contract value.
- **10 (now 11) Dispute Resolution — no escalation path or jurisdiction.** "Good-faith negotiation" alone gives a dissatisfied consumer no visible route to the National Consumer Commission, an ombud, or a specific court, which is a fairness/transparency gap. **Fixed this session** — added a reference to escalation to the National Consumer Commission, an accredited consumer ombud, or a court of competent jurisdiction.
- **No governing law / jurisdiction clause anywhere.** Recommend adding one line stating the agreement is governed by South African law and identifying which magistrate's court/high court division has jurisdiction, for clarity if a dispute ever needs to go further than negotiation.
- **No force majeure clause.** Supply-chain delays (import units, compressors) are common in HVAC — worth adding a short clause excusing delay caused by events outside your reasonable control, so 2.1's payment timing isn't read as a fixed guarantee you must meet regardless of supplier delay.

## What was changed in this session

1. Added clause 5.3 (warranty is additional to, not a substitute for, CPA rights).
2. Added a new "Errors and Omissions" section (6.1–6.3) with a mandatory gross-negligence/fraud/CPA carve-out.
3. Added a POPIA reference to the Confidentiality clause (now 9.1).
4. Added an escalation path (National Consumer Commission / ombud / court) to Dispute Resolution (now 11.1).
5. Renumbered all subsequent sections (Banking Details, Deposit Reference, Confidentiality, Termination, Dispute Resolution) to keep numbering sequential after inserting the new section.
